Mass maths

If mass is never lost, you can work out a missing mass with simple addition and subtraction.

How it works

Total mass of reactants = total mass of products

Worked example 1

hydrogen + oxygen → water

4.0 g of hydrogen reacts completely with 32.0 g of oxygen. What mass of water is made?

  1. Add up the reactants: 4.0 g + 32.0 g = 36.0 g
  2. Mass is conserved, so the products must also total 36.0 g.
  3. Water is the only product, so 36.0 g of water is made.

Worked example 2

calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide

10.0 g of calcium carbonate is heated in a closed system. It makes 5.6 g of calcium oxide. What mass of carbon dioxide is made?

  1. Total of the reactants: 10.0 g
  2. The products must total 10.0 g too.
  3. 10.0 g − 5.6 g = 4.4 g of carbon dioxide

Worked example 3: an open system

An open flask of acid and marble chips reads 150.0 g on a balance. When the fizzing stops it reads 148.2 g. What mass of carbon dioxide escaped?

  1. The mass that left the flask is the difference between the readings.
  2. 150.0 g − 148.2 g = 1.8 g of carbon dioxide escaped.
