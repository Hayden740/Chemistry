Seaford Secondary CollegeYear 9 Science: Chemical sciences

Nothing lost,nothing made.

In a chemical reaction, atoms swap partners but never disappear. In this lesson you will prove it, write it down as an equation, and balance it.

Start activity 1

Learning intentions

  • Understand that atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed, in a chemical reaction.
  • Learn how to represent reactions with word equations and symbol equations.
  • Learn how to balance a chemical equation.

Success criteria

I can:

  • explain the law of conservation of mass using the idea of atoms
  • predict and explain mass changes in open and closed systems
  • calculate a missing mass in a reaction
  • write a word equation from a description
  • count the atoms in a chemical formula
  • balance an equation by changing coefficients, not subscripts

Key words

Reactant
A substance you start with. Reactants go on the left of the arrow.
Product
A new substance made in the reaction. Products go on the right.
Law of conservation of mass
The total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.
Closed system
A setup nothing can get into or out of, such as a sealed bottle.
Open system
A setup where substances, especially gases, can enter or escape.
Chemical formula
Symbols and numbers that show the atoms in a substance, such as H2O.
Subscript
The small number after a symbol. It counts atoms inside one particle.
Coefficient
The big number in front of a formula. It counts whole particles.
Balanced equation
An equation with the same number of each type of atom on both sides.
